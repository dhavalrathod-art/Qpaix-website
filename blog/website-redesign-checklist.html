Speed, mobile, trust and clear calls to action: the 12 things to check before you redesign your website. In this article we share what we have learned from real projects with Indian businesses, and give you a simple plan you can start with this month.

Why this matters now

Customers expect faster service, teams are stretched thin and margins are tight. The right technology choice can remove hours of manual work every week, while the wrong one becomes an expensive habit. The difference usually comes down to asking a few good questions before you start.

Most of the businesses we work with do not need the biggest or newest tool. They need something that fits the way they already work, that their team will actually use, and that pays for itself quickly.

Three things to check first

  1. The problem in numbers. How many hours, mistakes or lost sales does it cost you each month today?
  2. Who will use it. A tool your team finds confusing will be ignored, however powerful it is.
  3. How you will measure success. Agree on one or two numbers before you spend a rupee.

A simple plan to get started

Pick one process that is repetitive, rules-based and painful today. Build the smallest version that solves it, put it in front of real users within a few weeks, and improve it based on their feedback.

  • Week 1: map the current process and agree on the goal
  • Weeks 2–4: design and build a first working version
  • Weeks 5–6: test with real users and fix what they find
  • Week 7 onwards: measure, improve and roll out to more people

"The best projects we have delivered all started small. The first version proved the value, and the business case for the next step wrote itself."

Dhaval Rathod, UI & Web Designer, Qpaix

Common mistakes to avoid

Trying to solve everything at once, skipping user testing, and choosing a technology before understanding the problem. Each of these adds months and lakhs to a project, and all of them are easy to avoid with a clear plan.

Key takeaways

  • Start with a real, measurable business problem
  • Keep the first version small and get it in front of users quickly
  • Measure results and grow step by step
